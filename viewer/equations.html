Equations of motion of the ballbot

A ballbot is a robot that stands on one ball. The body must stay upright.

This page derives the equations from free body diagrams. A free body diagram shows one chosen system and the external forces on that system.

Part 1 is one ballbot. Part 2 is two ballbots that hold one rigid bar. The words follow ASD-STE100, Simplified Technical English.

Part 1. One ballbot Part 2. Two ballbots and one bar

1. The robot

The positive direction is to the right. The model has one body and one ball.

2. The three coordinates

x is the position of the ball center on the floor. A positive value of x moves the ball to the right.

ψ is the spin of the ball. A positive value of ψ rolls the ball to the right.

θ is the lean angle of the body from the vertical line. A positive value of θ moves the body mass to the right.

The body mass center is l sin θ to the right of the ball center. The body mass center is l cos θ above the ball center.

A dot over a coordinate is the speed of that coordinate. Two dots over a coordinate are the acceleration of that coordinate.

Acceleration of the body mass center

The horizontal position of the body mass center is x + l sin θ. Take the rate of this position, and then the rate of that speed.

ax = ẍ + l cos θ · θ̈ − l sin θ · θ̇2

ax is the horizontal acceleration of the body mass center. The last term is the horizontal part of the acceleration along the circle of radius l.

The height of the body mass center above the ball center is l cos θ. The ball center does not move up or down.

az = −l sin θ · θ̈ − l cos θ · θ̇2

az is the vertical acceleration of the body mass center. Up is positive.

3. Free body diagram of the whole robot

The system is the ball and the body together. Draw only the forces that come from outside the system.

The floor applies f and N at the contact point. A positive value of f pushes the robot to the right. N pushes the robot up.

Gravity applies mg at the body mass center and mbg at the ball center.

The motor torque acts between the body and the ball. That torque is internal. The force between the body and the ball is internal. Internal forces do not appear on this diagram.

4. Horizontal equation, step by step

m is the mass of the body. mb is the mass of the ball.

  1. The only horizontal external force is f.
  2. The horizontal acceleration of the ball center is ẍ.
  3. The horizontal acceleration of the body mass center is ax.
  4. Mass times acceleration, for the two masses, equals the external force.
mb ẍ + m ax = f

Use the expression for ax from section 2.

mb ẍ + m (ẍ + l cos θ · θ̈ − l sin θ · θ̇2) = f

Collect the ẍ terms. Move the speed term to the right side.

(m + mb) ẍ + m l cos θ · θ̈ = m l sin θ · θ̇2 + f

This is the horizontal equation of the whole robot. The motor torque does not appear. The motor torque does not push the robot along the floor.

When the lean is positive, the circular motion accelerates the body mass toward the ball center, so that part of ax points left. On the right side of the equation that term is positive.

5. Normal force, step by step

  1. The vertical acceleration of the ball center is zero.
  2. The vertical acceleration of the body mass center is az.
  3. Up is positive. The external vertical forces are N, the body weight, and the ball weight.
N − (m + mb) g = m az
N = (m + mb) g + m az

Use az from section 2.

N = (m + mb) g − m l sin θ · θ̈ − m l cos θ · θ̇2

g is the gravity acceleration. When the body is upright and still, az is zero and N equals the total weight. When the body falls, N decreases. The ball leaves the floor when N is zero.

6. Free body diagram of the ball

Rx is the horizontal force of the ball on the body. A positive value of Rx pushes the body to the right. Rz is the vertical force of the ball on the body. A positive value of Rz pushes the body up.

On the ball, those forces have the opposite direction. The diagram shows them as −Rx and −Rz.

Spin of the ball

Take moments about the ball center. The positive moment has the same direction as positive ψ.

  1. Rx, Rz, and the ball weight act at the ball center. Their moment about the ball center is zero.
  2. The normal force acts on the vertical line through the ball center. Its moment about the ball center is zero.
  3. The friction force acts at a distance r below the ball center. A positive f makes a moment against the positive spin. The moment is r f.
  4. The motor torque τ has the positive spin direction. A positive value of τ rolls the ball to the right.
  5. Ib is the inertia of the ball about the ball center. Inertia times angular acceleration equals the sum of the moments.
Ib ψ̈ = τ − r f

7. Free body diagram of the body

Take moments about the body mass center. The positive moment increases θ.

  1. The weight acts at the mass center. Its moment about the mass center is zero.
  2. The ball center is l cos θ below the mass center. The ball center is l sin θ to the left of the mass center when θ is positive.
  3. Rx points to the right and acts below the mass center. This force decreases θ. Its moment is Rx l cos θ.
  4. Rz points up. When θ is positive, this force acts to the left of the mass center and increases θ. Its moment is Rz l sin θ.
  5. The torque on the body is −τ. I is the inertia of the body about the body mass center.
I θ̈ = −τ − Rx l cos θ + Rz l sin θ

Newton’s second law for the body mass center gives the two joint forces.

Rx = m ax = m (ẍ + l cos θ · θ̈ − l sin θ · θ̇2)
Rz − m g = m az
Rz = m g − m l sin θ · θ̈ − m l cos θ · θ̇2

Put these two forces into the moment equation.

I θ̈ = −τ − l cos θ · m (ẍ + l cos θ · θ̈ − l sin θ · θ̇2) + l sin θ · (m g − m l sin θ · θ̈ − m l cos θ · θ̇2)

Multiply. The two terms that contain θ̇2 are + m l2 sin θ cos θ · θ̇2 and − m l2 sin θ cos θ · θ̇2. Their sum is zero.

I θ̈ = −τ − m l cos θ · ẍ − m l2 θ̈ + m g l sin θ

Move the acceleration terms to the left side.

m l cos θ · ẍ + (I + m l2) θ̈ = m g l sin θ − τ

This is the lean equation. Gravity pulls the body mass down. A positive lean makes the gravity moment increase θ. A positive motor torque decreases θ.

8. Roll with no slip

The ball rolls with no slip when the contact point does not move on the floor.

ẋ = r ψ̇

The same relation is true for the accelerations: ẍ = r ψ̈.

  1. Solve the spin equation for the friction force: f = (τ − Ib ψ̈) / r.
  2. Replace ψ̈ with ẍ / r.
f = τ / r − (Ib / r2) ẍ

Put this f into the horizontal equation. Move the new ẍ term to the left side.

(m + mb + Ib / r2) ẍ + m l cos θ · θ̈ = m l sin θ · θ̇2 + τ / r

The lean equation has no f. It stays the same.

m l cos θ · ẍ + (I + m l2) θ̈ = m g l sin θ − τ

The balance controller uses these two equations. The no-slip equation is true only when f is between −μ N and μ N. μ is the friction coefficient of the floor.

9. When the ball slips

The ball slips when the friction for no slip is larger than μ N.

The slip speed is ẋ − r ψ̇. When the ball slips, the friction force has magnitude μ N. The friction force has the opposite direction of the slip.

f = −μ N · sign(ẋ − r ψ̇)

Use this force in the three equations of sections 4, 6, and 7. Do not use the two no-slip equations.

11. Two ballbots and one bar

Robot A stands on the left. Robot B stands on the right. A rigid bar joins the two handles. This is the planar model of the two robots in the plane of the bar.

Each robot has the same mass and the same length as the robot in part 1. The bar has mass mo and length L. The mass center of the bar is at the middle of the bar.

Each handle is a pin on the body axis, at distance h from the ball center. A pin applies a force. A pin applies no moment. The two leans have equal magnitude and opposite sign, so the two handles stay at one height and the bar stays horizontal.

12. Free body diagram of the whole system

The total mass is the two robots plus the bar.

M = 2(m + mb) + mo
  1. xG is the horizontal position of the mass center of this whole system.
  2. The horizontal external forces are fA and fB.
  3. The vertical external forces are NA, NB, and the total weight Mg.
M ẍG = fA + fB

Each floor has its own friction limit.

|fA| ≤ μA NA
|fB| ≤ μB NB

The largest horizontal force on the whole system is the sum of the two limits. That sum limits ẍG.

When no mass accelerates up or down, the vertical equation is

NA + NB = M g

13. Cut the bar

The bar is a separate system. The hand forces are external to the bar.

For a bar that stays horizontal and does not rotate, and whose mass center is in the middle:

HA + HB = mo a
VA + VB = mo g
VA = VB = mo g / 2

a is the horizontal acceleration of the bar. In the steady carry, the bar, the two ball centers, and the system mass center have this same acceleration a. The leans are constant.

Write the horizontal share as a common part plus a compression S.

HA = mo a / 2 + S
HB = mo a / 2 − S

A positive value of S compresses the bar. The two compression terms sum to zero, so compression does not accelerate the bar.

14. One robot, with the hand force

The force of the bar on the robot is the opposite of the force of the robot on the bar.

Px = −HA
Pz = −VA = −mo g / 2

Use the horizontal result of section 4, and add Px. For a constant lean, ax equals a.

(m + mb) a = fA + Px = fA − HA

Solve for the friction force. Do the same step for robot B.

fA = (m + mb + mo / 2) a + S
fB = (m + mb + mo / 2) a − S

The term with a accelerates that robot and half of the bar. The term S compresses the bar. Both terms are in the same friction force, so both must fit in the cone.

|(m + mb + mo / 2) a + S| ≤ μA NA
|(m + mb + mo / 2) a − S| ≤ μB NB

At rest, a is zero. Then fA = S and fB = −S. The compression cannot exceed either cone. The smaller floor sets the limit.

With no compression, S is zero. The two friction forces are equal. Their sum is M a, which matches section 12.

The vertical equation for robot A, with no vertical acceleration, is

NA = (m + mb) g − Pz = (m + mb) g + mo g / 2

The bar pushes down on the handle. This load increases NA. A larger normal force allows a larger friction force.

15. The lean at rest

At rest, the acceleration and the speed are zero. Take moments of the external forces on robot A about the ball center. The sum of the moments is zero. The positive moment increases θ.

  1. The body weight has moment m g l sin θ.
  2. The friction force has moment −r fA.
  3. The hand force has moment h cos θ · Px − h sin θ · Pz.
  4. The motor torque is internal to the robot. It does not appear in this sum.
  5. From the horizontal equation at rest, fA = −Px.
m g l sin θ = −Px (r + h cos θ) + Pz h sin θ

For a small lean and no vertical hand force, sin θ ≈ θ and cos θ ≈ 1.

θ ≈ −Px (r + h) / (m g l)

During a compression, the bar pushes robot A to the left, so Px is negative and θ is positive. Robot A leans to the right, toward the bar. Robot B leans to the left, toward the bar.

The spin equation of each ball is the same equation as in section 6. The hand force acts on the body, so it does not add a moment on the ball about the ball center. At rest, τ = r f.
