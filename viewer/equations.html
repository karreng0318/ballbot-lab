Equations of motion of the ballbot

A ballbot is a robot that stands on one ball. The body must stay upright.

This page shows the equations of motion of the planar model. The words follow ASD-STE100, Simplified Technical English.

1. The robot

The planar model looks at the robot from the side. The model has one body and one ball.

The positive direction is to the right.

2. The three coordinates

The model uses three coordinates.

x is the position of the ball center on the floor. A positive value of x moves the ball to the right.

ψ is the spin of the ball. A positive value of ψ rolls the ball to the right.

θ is the lean angle of the body from the vertical line. A positive value of θ moves the body mass to the right.

The body mass center is l sin θ to the right of the ball center. The body mass center is l cos θ above the ball center.

A dot over a coordinate is the speed of that coordinate. Two dots over a coordinate are the acceleration of that coordinate.

3. The forces and the torque

The motor applies the torque τ to the ball. The body receives the torque minus τ.

A positive value of τ rolls the ball to the right. A positive value of τ leans the body to the left.

The floor applies two forces to the ball. f is the friction force. A positive value of f pushes the ball to the right.

N is the normal force. N pushes the ball up.

4. The three equations

m is the mass of the body. mb is the mass of the ball.

Horizontal motion of the ball and the body:

(m + mb) ẍ + m l cos θ · θ̈ = m l sin θ · θ̇2 + f

The left side is the horizontal acceleration of the two masses. f is the only horizontal force on the robot from the floor.

The motor torque acts between the body and the ball. This torque does not push the robot along the floor.

Spin of the ball about the ball center:

Ib ψ̈ = τ − r f

Ib is the inertia of the ball about the ball center. The friction force acts at a distance r below the ball center. This force makes the torque r f on the ball.

Rotation of the body about the ball center:

m l cos θ · ẍ + (I + m l2) θ̈ = m g l sin θ − τ

I is the inertia of the body about the body mass center. g is the gravity acceleration.

Gravity pulls the body mass down. A positive lean angle makes this gravity torque increase θ.

5. The normal force

The ball center does not move up or down. Only the body mass center can move up or down.

z̈ = −l sin θ · θ̈ − l cos θ · θ̇2
N = (m + mb) g + m z̈

z̈ is the vertical acceleration of the body mass center. When the body falls, N decreases.

The ball leaves the floor when N is zero.

6. Roll with no slip

The ball rolls with no slip when the contact point does not move on the floor.

ẋ = r ψ̇

You can remove f and ψ from the three equations when this equation is true. Two equations in x and θ remain:

(m + mb + Ib / r2) ẍ + m l cos θ · θ̈ = m l sin θ · θ̇2 + τ / r
m l cos θ · ẍ + (I + m l2) θ̈ = m g l sin θ − τ

The balance controller uses these two equations.

The no-slip equation is true only when f is between −μ N and μ N. μ is the friction coefficient of the floor.

7. When the ball slips

The ball slips when the friction for no slip is larger than μ N.

The slip speed is ẋ − r ψ̇. When the ball slips, the friction force is μ N. The friction force has the opposite direction of the slip.

f = −μ N · sign(ẋ − r ψ̇)

Use this force in the three equations of section 4. Do not use the two no-slip equations.
